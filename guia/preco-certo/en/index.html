How it works

Three prices per item, no jargon

🧱 Minimum price

É o floor. It covers the cost, the commission, the fee, all taxes on the sale, and the item's share of fixed expenses, and nothing is left over. Below it, the sale does not cover its share of the overhead.

🎯 Target price

É o price that gives the margin the client wants (it picks: 15%, 20%…). It is the target for your next price increases.

✂️ Maximum discount

É o salesperson's limit: how much you can lower today's price without leaving the target margin. If the price is already below the target, the maximum discount is zero.

🧮 How the calculation is done

This is the markup that accountants know: the price covers the cost, the commission and the fee, the other taxes on the sale (ISS or ICMS, which continue in 2027, and the IRPJ/CSLL under Presumido), the share of fixed expenses (allocated by revenue, or by another single percentage you enter), and the consumption tax. Under Lucro Presumido and Lucro Real, the kit compares today's PIS/Cofins with the 2027 CBS and IBS. In 2027, the CBS is the reference rate minus 0.1 point, and the IBS is 0.1%, so the two add up to the reference rate itself. The new tax applies to the price, with a credit on the portion of the cost that comes from purchases with the tax shown separately on the invoice. If you don't enter that portion item by item, the kit uses the company's own proportion, the same rule as the Diagnostic. Under Simples, the tax is the effective DAS rate.

⚠️ What it is NOT

  • It is not the price the market will accept. It's the price that gives you the margin. If the market won't pay it, the conversation is about cost or about no longer selling the item.
  • It doesn't have the final rate. That is why it uses two scenarios and marks 2027 as SIMULATED.
  • The base is simplified: CBS and IBS are charged "on top" of the price, but the kit treats the tax as part of the stated price, which makes the calculation slightly heavier (conservative). Items with reduced rates, tax substitution, or a specific regime need the accountant's review.
  • Under Lucro Real, IR/CSLL is levied on profit, and here it enters as a fraction of revenue: it is an approximation.
  • The 2027 margin assumes the same total price for the customer. Those who sell to businesses can pass part of the CBS and IBS on "separately," because the buyer uses the tax as a credit: in that case the margin drop is smaller. That's a conversation to have with the client.

The proof

We run on two fictional examples

The Diagnostic's Presumed Profit (Lucro Presumido) agency and a Simples electrical supplies store, with made-up data (11/25/2026). The 2027 numbers are SIMULATED.

-5,8%is the ad campaign's margin today for the agency: with its share of fixed expenses, it already operates at a loss. In 2027, -11.0%.
R$ 4.860,31is the campaign's target price in 2027, in the 11.3% scenario. Today it charges R$ 2.500,00: either the price goes up, the cost goes down, or it comes off the menu.
R$ 19,38is the target price of the Simples store's light bulb, which today sells for R$ 14.90, with commission, card fee, and DAS.
  1. The agency's other services have room to spare.
    The consulting service can take up to 52.3% in discounts without falling below the target margin; the campaign can't take any. The average of the item margins, weighted by revenue, matches the company's margin (about 19.8%).
  2. Under Simples, what you see is the price including the DAS.
    All 4 of the store's items fall below the 12% target margin, as does the store itself (7% margin). The effect of the Reform only comes in if it opts to pay CBS and IBS outside the DAS (kit 2).
  3. The same number in both kits.
    The campaign's target price is the same in the Diagnostic and here, because both use the same calculation.
  4. 10 tests check the calculations.
    Target price and minimum price return the right margin, the average of the margins matches the company's, the maximum discount, Simples with commission, fee, and DAS, and input errors.
